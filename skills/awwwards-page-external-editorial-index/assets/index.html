A place to begin

Practice / 12 Oct 2026 · Mara Vale

We cleared one corner of the table and left the rest as it was. A notebook lay beside an unfinished drawing. The first task was simply to write the question we wanted to answer.

By the end of the afternoon there was no finished object. There was a useful beginning: a few measurements, two mistakes we understood and a reason to return tomorrow.

The shape of quiet

Material / 3 Oct 2026 · Tomas Reed

The bowl was shallow and slightly uneven. In the shop it had seemed almost without detail. At home its rim caught a narrow line of light each morning.

We began to notice the space around it. The shelf held fewer things, and each object had room to be understood. A quiet shape had changed the whole arrangement.

Across a familiar bridge

Places / 25 Sep 2026 · Iris North

For years the bridge had been part of the quickest route into town. One Saturday we crossed it slowly and stopped at the centre to look upstream.

The view was ordinary, which was part of its value. We could return in another season and find the same banks holding different colours, water and light.

Notes from the workbench

Practice / 12 Sep 2026 · Mara Vale

A piece of tape marked the setting that had worked. Next to it we wrote down the setting that had not. Neither note was elegant, but both saved time the following week.

When another person joined the work, the notes offered a way in. They could see what had been tried and choose the next question without guessing at our intentions.

A surface that remembers

Material / 2 Sep 2026 · Tomas Reed

The handle had become smooth where a hand rested most often. Elsewhere the wood still held small marks from the tool that shaped it.

We kept both kinds of surface. One recorded the making and the other recorded use. Together they gave an ordinary object a precise, readable history.

Reading a changing shoreline

Places / 20 Aug 2026 · Iris North

A line of stones we had drawn on the map was covered when we returned. The tide had made a different path, leaving a strip of pale sand farther along the bay.

We added a date to the drawing. It was no longer a promise about where the shore would be. It was a record of what we had found on one particular morning.

A useful second attempt

Practice / 5 Aug 2026 · Mara Vale

The first frame leaned to one side. Instead of discarding it, we measured the difference and made a second frame with a single joint adjusted.

Comparing the two made the result easier to understand. The change was small enough to name, and the lesson was specific enough to use again.

The old window did not make a perfectly clear view. Its surface bent the edges of the garden into a soft, irregular pattern.

As the afternoon moved, the pattern crossed the table. We made three quick drawings and kept the differences between them rather than choosing a single best version.

A walk without a destination

Places / 10 Jul 2026 · Iris North

We left the map folded and turned at the next path that looked inviting. A gate, a repair in a wall and a patch of shade became reasons to stop.

The walk took less than an hour. Its record filled several pages because we had given small details the time we usually reserved for a destination.

Leaving room in the margin

Practice / 15 Jun 2026 · Mara Vale

The plan was nearly ready to share, but the page was too full. We moved the drawing inward and left a wide margin around it.

At the meeting the margin filled with questions. The extra space had not weakened the proposal. It had made participation a visible part of the work.

At the edge of the orchard

Places / 30 May 2026 · Iris North

At the last row of trees, the clipped ground gave way to a taller strip of grass. Beyond it a narrow road followed the hill.

We sat where the three edges met. The place was useful because none of its parts stood alone: the orchard, the verge and the road each explained something about the others.

Paper after the rain

Material / Date not recorded · Tomas Reed

The sketch had dried into gentle folds. Some pencil lines remained clear; others had disappeared into a pale wash along the edge.

There was no date on the sheet. We kept that part of the record unknown and described only what the paper could still show us.
